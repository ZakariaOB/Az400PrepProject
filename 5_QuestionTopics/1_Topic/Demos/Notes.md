
## Burn up and burn down charts 

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fDhAfh7zlDo

- The Burndown Chart and Burnup Chart are both tools used in Agile project management to track progress and help teams visualize their work, but they present information differently and focus on different aspects of progress. Here's a breakdown of the differences:

1. Burndown Chart
    Purpose: Tracks the amount of work remaining in a sprint or project over time.
    Focus: Shows how much work is left to do and whether the team is on track to complete it within the timeline.
    Axes:
        X-axis: Time (e.g., days, sprints, or iterations).
        Y-axis: Remaining work (e.g., story points, tasks, or hours).
    Key Line:
        A declining line indicates progress toward completion. Ideally, it approaches zero as the sprint or project ends.
    Use Case:
        Useful for understanding how quickly the team is burning through the backlog and whether they are on track to meet deadlines.
    Example:
        A team starts with 50 story points at the beginning of a sprint, and the chart shows how those points decrease day by day.

2. Burnup Chart
    Purpose: Tracks the amount of work completed and compares it to the total scope of work.
    Focus: Shows progress made as well as how much work remains, allowing for adjustments to scope if necessary.
    Axes:
        X-axis: Time (e.g., days, sprints, or iterations).
        Y-axis: Work (e.g., story points, tasks, or hours).
    Key Lines:
        A rising line represents the amount of work completed.
        A horizontal or rising scope line represents the total work to be done.
    Use Case:
        Useful for visualizing both progress and scope changes over time. It helps teams understand how scope adjustments (e.g., adding or removing features) impact delivery.

Example:

    A project with 100 total story points starts with 20 points completed, and the chart shows how the completed work increases over time while the total scope might increase or decrease due to changes.

# Key Differences

| **Aspect**        | **Burndown Chart**                       | **Burnup Chart**                         |
|--------------------|------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| **Focus**         | Work remaining                          | Work completed and total scope           |
| **Visualization** | Declining line                          | Rising lines for progress and scope      |
| **Scope Changes** | Not visible                             | Clearly visible                          |
| **Insights**      | Tracks progress toward completion       | Tracks progress and accommodates changes |
| **Use Case**      | Sprint or short-term progress tracking  | Long-term tracking with scope changes    |

---

## Summary
- Use a **Burndown Chart** if your main focus is tracking how much work remains and staying on top of sprint goals.
- Use a **Burnup Chart** if you want a broader view that includes progress made and potential scope changes over time.
